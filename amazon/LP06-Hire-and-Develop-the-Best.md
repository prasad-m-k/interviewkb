# LP06 - Hire and Develop the Best

> Leaders raise the performance bar with every hire and promotion. They recognize exceptional talent, and willingly move them throughout the organization. Leaders develop leaders and take seriously their role in coaching others. We work on behalf of our people to invent mechanisms for development like Career Choice.

---

## STAR Format

**Situation:** At HPE, I was a senior engineer on a team that supported global enterprise customers across multiple product lines. We had an influx of new engineers coming from support backgrounds who were technically capable but had no exposure to system-level architecture thinking. They could solve tickets but could not yet diagnose root cause patterns across products.

**Task:** As one of the more experienced engineers, I was informally asked by the team lead to help onboard and develop two junior engineers who were showing strong potential but were struggling with the architectural reasoning layer.

**Action:** I designed a structured pairing approach over six weeks. Each week, I walked through a real case with each engineer — not to give them the answer, but to narrate the diagnostic reasoning out loud. I created a shared document where we captured the "why behind the fix" for each case, which eventually became a reference that other engineers started using. I also introduced them to system thinking frameworks I had developed over years — how to trace failures across layers, how to identify whether a problem was environmental, configurational, or architectural.

**Result:** Both engineers became significantly more independent within two quarters. One of them went on to become a technical lead for a product workstream. The shared reasoning document evolved into a team knowledge base that persisted long after the formal pairing ended.

## SOAR Format

**Situation:** Two junior HPE engineers had strong support instincts but no framework for architectural reasoning. Their escalation rates were high and their root-cause analyses were shallow, which created bottlenecks for the senior engineers on the team.

**Obstacle:** There was no formal mentorship program. My own workload was full, and carving out structured development time meant accepting personal overhead with no formal recognition attached to it.

**Action:** I committed six weeks to structured pairing, narrating my diagnostic reasoning aloud on real cases rather than just fixing and moving on. I built a shared "why behind the fix" document that externalized the reasoning so it could outlast the pairing.

**Result:** Both engineers grew into independent contributors. The knowledge base document became a team resource. One engineer later moved into a technical lead role. The investment paid forward in reduced escalation volume to senior staff for the following two years.
