# LP05 - Learn and Be Curious

> Leaders are never done learning and always seek to improve themselves. They are curious about new possibilities and act on them.

---

## STAR Format

**Situation:** In 2024, the enterprise AI landscape shifted fast enough that my existing mental model — shaped by years in HP/HPE infrastructure and OpenText operations tooling — was no longer sufficient to advise clients or architect modern solutions. I was competent in the tooling of the prior decade but not in the LLM-native patterns emerging in production environments.

**Task:** I needed to close the gap systematically, not just consume articles. The goal was to build enough depth to contribute original thinking, not just apply vendor patterns.

**Action:** I pursued multiple learning tracks in parallel. I built a voice interview agent using Claude CLI and a structured knowledge base covering CI/CD, DevSecOps, GitOps, and DSA. I developed and published four independent research papers on SSRN covering escalation theory in sociotechnical systems, agentic AI governance, shadow AI risk, and nonprofit AI challenges. I designed and built a full GCP-based agentic system for RFP response generation using Vertex AI, LangGraph, and Firestore. I also began publishing on Medium to externalize my applied learning, with a running series on LLM evaluation metrics and escalation engineering.

**Result:** Within twelve months, I moved from consumer of AI tooling to architect of agentic systems, published researcher, and public writer on the topic. The SSRN profile now has four indexed working papers. The Medium series has a growing readership. The agentic systems I built have moved beyond proof-of-concept into patent-disclosure stage.

---

## SOAR Format

**Situation:** After 25 years of building expertise in infrastructure, support tooling, and enterprise operations, I recognized that the AI wave was not an incremental shift — it was a foundational change in how systems would be designed and operated. My existing skills were valuable but not sufficient.

**Obstacle:** The learning curve was steep and the signal-to-noise ratio in the field was poor. Most content was shallow. Building real depth meant doing original work, not just consuming it.

**Action:** I structured my learning around building and writing. I built an agentic RFP system, a voice interview agent, and a runbook decomposition prototype. I wrote four research papers and began a public writing practice on Medium. Each project forced me to understand a layer of the technology I could not have accessed by reading alone.

**Result:** The depth I built through doing translated directly into original contributions: publishable research, patentable architectures, and the ability to advise on agentic AI at a level most practitioners cannot yet reach.
