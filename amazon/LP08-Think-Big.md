# LP08 - Think Big

> Thinking small is a self-fulfilling prophecy. Leaders create and communicate a bold direction that inspires results. They think differently and look around corners to shape the future.

---

## STAR Format

**Situation:** The nonprofit and public sector AI conversation in 2024 was dominated by two narratives: either AI would revolutionize everything, or it was inaccessible to resource-constrained organizations. Neither framing helped the organizations actually trying to figure out what to do Monday morning.

**Task:** I wanted to contribute something more useful than hype or caution. My civic involvement through organizations like Suvidha and 100 Black Men gave me direct exposure to how nonprofits were actually experiencing the AI transition — as a widening capability gap between well-resourced institutions and everyone else.

**Action:** I wrote "Governing AI in Resource-Constrained Environments" as a research paper that reframed the problem around governance infrastructure, not tool adoption. The argument was that the real risk was not that nonprofits would misuse AI, but that they would either avoid it entirely or adopt it without any policy scaffolding — creating invisible liability and eroding the trust of the communities they served. I introduced a tiered governance model specifically designed for organizations with minimal technical staff. I also adapted the paper into an SSIR-targeted article, "The Vulnerability Gap," to reach the practitioner audience directly.

**Result:** The paper is indexed on SSRN and represents one of the few frameworks specifically designed for nonprofit AI governance rather than ported from enterprise contexts. The SSIR-targeted version is ready for submission to reach program officers and executive directors who are making these decisions right now.

---

## SOAR Format

**Situation:** Nonprofits and civic organizations were caught between AI adoption pressure and a complete absence of governance frameworks built for their context. Enterprise AI governance models assumed legal teams, compliance budgets, and technical staff that most nonprofits do not have.

**Obstacle:** The problem was not being addressed in the research literature. Most AI governance work focused on large organizations or government. Writing for nonprofits specifically meant building the framework from first principles with no existing literature to build on.

**Action:** I drew on direct civic experience from Suvidha and 100 Black Men to ground the framework in real operating conditions. I developed a tiered governance model that scaled to organizations with one IT person, no dedicated AI staff, and limited policy capacity. I produced both a research paper for the academic record and an article version for the practitioner audience.

**Result:** The framework fills a genuine gap in the literature. The SSRN paper is indexed and publicly available. The practitioner article is positioned for submission to SSIR, which reaches the exact decision-makers who need a governance starting point they can actually use.
