## STAR Format — Attributing Credit I Could Have Kept Quiet

**Situation:** My research on Escalation Threshold Theory (the SET model) built heavily on Professor Robert A. Pape's work on escalation in international conflict. Borrowing academic frameworks across domains is common — but attribution is where credibility either holds or breaks.

**Task:** I had to publish the paper and a related Medium series in a way that was intellectually honest about the framework's origin, while clearly stating what my own contribution actually was. The easier path was to let the attribution stay vague and let readers assume more originality than was there.

**Action:** I included an explicit closing attribution crediting Pape by name for the nomenclature and framing, and separately laid out where my contribution diverged: applying the model to technical and organizational escalation, adding the AI agent cascade indicator (TPI-AI), and operationalizing it for engineering environments. I didn't obscure the borrowed foundation — I built on it transparently.

**Result:** The attribution strengthened the paper rather than weakening it. Readers who knew Pape's work recognized the foundation immediately and could evaluate my extension on its own merits.

**Decision · Reasoning · Outcome**
- **Decision:** Name the source explicitly rather than leave the attribution ambiguous.
- **Reasoning:** Vagueness might have made the work look more original short-term, but it's the kind of thing that erodes trust the moment someone familiar with the source notices — and peer credibility depends on that not happening.
- **Outcome:** A stronger, more evaluable paper, and a research practice that holds up under scrutiny rather than around it.

**Quantified impact** *(illustrative estimate — replace with real numbers before using live)*
- Zero credibility challenges or correction requests post-publication.
- Readers familiar with Pape's original work engaged directly with the extension (TPI-AI indicator) rather than disputing the framing — the attribution pre-empted the one objection that could have undermined the whole paper.

**Why this reads L6/L7:** Protecting your own research credibility to the standard a peer-reviewer or EB1A judge would apply — not because anyone was checking, but because that's the bar — is a Staff-level self-governance signal, the same instinct that keeps a technical design honest when no one else is positioned to catch a gap.
