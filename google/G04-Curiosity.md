## STAR Format — Rebuilding My Mental Model for the AI Shift

**Situation:** In 2024, the enterprise AI landscape shifted fast enough that my existing mental model — built over years in HP/HPE infrastructure and OpenText operations tooling — was no longer sufficient to advise clients or architect modern solutions.

**Task:** I needed to close the gap systematically, with the goal of contributing original thinking rather than just applying vendor patterns secondhand.

**Action:** I pursued several learning tracks in parallel, all built around doing rather than reading: a voice interview agent using Claude CLI, a structured knowledge base spanning CI/CD, DevSecOps, GitOps, and DSA, four independent research papers on SSRN, and a full GCP-based agentic RFP system using Vertex AI, LangGraph, and Firestore. I started publishing on Medium to externalize the learning as it happened.

**Result:** Within twelve months I moved from consumer of AI tooling to architect of agentic systems, published researcher, and public writer — four SSRN-indexed papers and an agentic system that reached patent-disclosure stage.

**Decision · Reasoning · Outcome**
- **Decision:** Learn by building and publishing, not by consuming content.
- **Reasoning:** The field's signal-to-noise ratio was poor; real depth required doing original work that forced understanding, not passive reading.
- **Outcome:** Original contributions (publishable research, patentable architecture) that reading alone wouldn't have produced.

**Quantified impact** *(illustrative estimate — replace with real numbers before using live)*
- Cut your own solution-design cycle time for new GCP agentic architectures from an estimated 6 weeks to under 2 weeks, by reusing patterns built during the self-directed learning sprint.
- The four SSRN papers combined placed in the top decile of downloads for the agentic-AI/AI-governance category within 60–90 days of posting.

**Why this reads L6/L7:** The learning didn't stay personal — it produced reusable IP (patterns, frameworks, publications) that compressed delivery time on later projects. That's the difference between "I learned a lot" and "I converted learning into an organizational asset."
